About me

Hello! I'm Yihang Fan, a Doctoral Researcher at the University of California, Irvine. Driven by a passion for discovery, my research focuses on:

  • Cardiovascular disease risk assessment and prevention, with a focus on lipoprotein(a), lipid disorders, and coronary artery calcium (CAC).
  • Cardiometabolic health and disparities, including type 2 diabetes, obesity, CKM syndrome, and GLP-1-based therapies.
  • Causal inference and longitudinal analysis of prospective cohorts and electronic health records to advance cardiovascular prevention.

When I'm not working, you can find me exploring new cultures through travel, experimenting with flavors in the kitchen, or practicing the violin, a lifelong passion I have been honing since the age of four.

I am driven by the belief that true medicine looks beyond the data to understand the person. I dedicate my career to academic discovery not just to treat disease, but to proactively foster global longevity and help diverse communities thrive.

RESEARCH

Lipoprotein(a), Family History, and Incidence of Premature ASCVD Events Pooled Cohort Study

04/2024 - 07/2025

Harmonized Lp(a) and covariate definitions across five NIH-funded U.S. cohorts (ARIC, CARDIA, MESA, JHS, FHSOffspring; >27,000 participants, ~21-year follow-up), applying cohort-specific standardization and percentile-based categorization, then ran pooled multivariable Cox proportional-hazards models accounting for between-cohort heterogeneity.

My experience includes developing analytic cohorts and data workflows using SQL, SAS, R, and Python. In this work, I regularly draw on the UC Health Data Warehouse, TriNetX, UK Biobank, and NIH-supported cardiovascular cohorts, while exploring patient cohorts using Epic Cosmos.




Impact of an Electronic Health Record Best Practice Advisory on Lipoprotein(a) Testing and Downstream Lipid Management in a UC Health System Population

07/2025 - present

Led a quasi-experimental evaluation of a provider-facing Best Practice Advisory (BPA) prompting Lp(a) testing for high-risk patients, comparing 610 BPA-exposed patients at UCI Health against eligible controls at other UC health systems using UC Health Data Warehouse EHR data.

Built the causal-inference pipeline in databricks: propensity-score matching, standardized-mean-difference balance diagnostics, and doubly robust logistic regression with IPTW/AIPW sensitivity analyses and cluster-robust standard errors.

Found BPA exposure raised Lp(a) testing within 12 months from 3.8% to 46.1% versus usual care, providing real-world evidence that targeted EHR decision support closes guideline-care gaps at scale.




Defining the Optimal Range of Lipoprotein(a) for Cardiovascular Disease and Type 2 Diabetes Risk in the UK Biobank

07/2025 - present

Designed a prospective cohort study of 397,930 UK Biobank participants, using R and Python to process linked EHR and biomarker data and model nonlinear exposure-outcome relationships for incident ASCVD and type 2 diabetes (multivariable Cox regression and restricted cubic splines, with sex- and age-stratified interaction testing) over a median 16.6-year follow-up.

Identified a low-intermediate Lp(a) range (<30 nmol/L) with the lowest combined cardiometabolic risk (ASCVD HR 1.37 [95% CI 1.32-1.42] at ≥125 vs <5 nmol/L; modest inverse association with type 2 diabetes); first-authored manuscript under revision at the Journal of the American College of Cardiology.




Understanding Disparities in Glycemic Control and Cardiovascular Risk Among Hispanic and Non-Hispanic Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Patients Using UC Health Data Warehouse

07/2025 - present

Designed a longitudinal real-world evidence cohort using large-scale EHR data from the UC Health Data Warehouse, utilizing SQL, R, and Python to extract, clean, and structure electronic medical records, to examine glycemic control, medication use, and cardiovascular outcomes among Hispanic and non-Hispanic patients with type 2 diabetes.

Modeled medication prescription patterns, adherence (PDC ≥80%), and HbA1c trajectories using multivariable regression and time-to-event analyses to evaluate disparities in diabetes management across health systems.

Assessed the association between poor glycemic control and incident cardiovascular events, applying Cox proportional hazards models and mediation frameworks to examine the roles of medication adherence, treatment patterns, and social determinants of health (SDOH).
